Beam math

A flashlight box leads with lumens because lumens are the big number, but lumens say nothing about reach. Throw lives in the candela, hours live in the watt-hours, and the wait lives in the charger. Beam math works all three honestly: candela becomes meters by the ANSI throw rule, the cell's watt-hours become real runtime at honest system efficiency, and the charge becomes the hours it actually takes. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

The three tools

  • Throw plan - the candela becomes the meters the beam reaches before it fades to moonlight.
  • Runtime plan - the lumens against the cell's watt-hours become the hours the light really runs.
  • Charge plan - the cell and the charger's milliamps become the honest wait at the outlet.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: the ANSI throw rule (twice the square root of candela), 80 lumens per watt at the system level and 85% charge efficiency - all starting points you replace by reading your own spec sheet. Real lights step down as they heat: the box number is the first minute, not the last. Never shine a powerful light at people, drivers or aircraft, and match the cell to what the light's manual allows.
